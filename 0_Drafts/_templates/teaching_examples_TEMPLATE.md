This document shows how feedback works in practice in COURSE CODE. Student work is reproduced anonymously as **Student A**; appended documents have been redacted to remove names and personal links.

## The assignment

What the assignment asks for, when in the semester it falls, and what students are given up front. If there is a published rubric, say so and summarize what it weights — a standard published in advance is the point worth making.

## Student A's submission

Reproduce the student's work verbatim in blockquotes. If the assignment has repeated prompts across multiple parts, state the prompts once in a numbered list and then use short bold labels above each response, rather than repeating the full prompt text.

## My response

> Your feedback, verbatim.

One or two sentences on what the feedback was trying to do. The useful observation is usually about your approach rather than this specific student — for example, giving students something to think about instead of a simple approve or reject.

## Outcome

What happened next: the revision, the final submission, the grade, or the skill the student carried forward. If a graded document is appended, say what it demonstrates rather than restating its contents. Note where the work was strong and where the rubric did the work for you.
